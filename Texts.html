Ideas

How do you get to love writing?

First of all, you have to ask yourself the question of how to love at all. As silly as it may sound, love can be defined, refined and worked on. Although strictly speaking, it is not rational work, it is through discipline that love is expressed. It is conceived in the deepest part of us as something vast that comes from the realm of the social, of the connection to the other—which can be justified by the social nature of human beings, but I am not dealing here with a biologist's argument. Love can be seen quite universally as a connection to others. Discipline affects the way this connection is established. It is therefore a question of the use of love, meaning, what lies between the subject and the object of love, which amounts to working the motivations, methods and consequences of a love that is always capable of maintaining a balance between subject and object. Thus, if love is not at once a motivation, a method and a consequence of its own making, it is much more likely that we are dealing with narcissism.

To love writing (constrained here to academic writing), one has to love the problem, the question, the mystery one tries to unravel. The fog in which one wanders has to be a place of wonder, and we have to see each new clue as if it were a magic trick. One cannot love writing if one does not know how to wonder. Secondly, one needs to love the process of making, crafting, working and articulating the elements of research. One needs to be passionate about material production. One cannot love writing if one does not know how to make art. Finally, one should love the game offered by the finished text, in other words the power it gives to its readers. This means working on the situation of the text, knowing the biggest machines of which it can be a part, what gears it can turn, and more concretely, what historical, political and individual impact it can have. These can only be hypotheses, and thus, one ought to be cautious when delimiting the boundaries of writing. One cannot love writing if one cannot know its power. To love writing, one has to be amazed, creative and aware of its power; one has to be a child, an artist and a politician all at the same time.


On Hating Identity. Hate this world and love this life.

We of course work mainly on identity, queer and diverse identities. But we should hate these identities, for they are not ours, they are those of a rotten world, of rotten institutions, of rotten authorities. We use these identities as mirrors of our souls, as the reflection of the human being behind the mask, because we are left with a world of representations, but we should not forget that we love the player and not the role. Loving identity is to fall into the narcissism of imperialism, of capitalism. We should love differences and multiplicity, we should love how a single human builds bridges between worlds to travel from the self to the other. We need these identities to communicate and organise, but we should aim at destroying these identities in a big Carnival fire.

Do not let others decide where the theatre, the spectators, and the actors are. We are actors and we are the spectators at all times, in a chaotic, disorganised, unstable feast. We are players. Irony is our best friend here; it allows us to take the unserious seriously and make meaningful the meaningless. It is the best friend to a world where crises of meaning scream everywhere, from the depths of ourselves to the surface of the whole planet. We are offered a meaningless world, and we need to work hard not to constrain it to the mediocre imaginary of the handful of powerful people. Do we want a world of spaceships, teslas, open spaces, amazon hangars, military drones, mechanical war dogs, jails and concentration camps? Because this is the imaginary that we’re following and producing if we let them shape our identities.

I can’t say I’m strong enough to fight, and I cannot mobilise people to burn the world to the ground. All the contrary: I am a fervent defender of love and justice, of smiles and transformations. I’m not very fond of the dead pope, but I agree that the minimum effort is to smile and to have a sense of humour. Smile at the world like a demon, and smile at the others like an angel. Hope that something good will come out of the ashes. They will burn from our love before we burn from their nightmares.
